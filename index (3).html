I’m Sapal Yonghang, an events and hospitality professional with an MSc in International Event Management, awarded with commendation, from Ulster University. My experience spans live event delivery in Dubai and conference operations in the UK, with a focus on planning, coordination and smooth execution on the day.

In Dubai, I worked on large-scale MICE and spectator events, including the Dubai World Cup for 3,000+ attendees, coordinating guest flow, seating and floor operations, preparing event signage, positioning equipment and liaising with suppliers during setup. In Belfast, I supported the CABS Annual Conference, registering and checking in 200+ delegates, and the Gastronomy Summit at Ulster University, where I handled speaker check-in, prepared rooms and kept sessions running to schedule.

I strengthened my planning skills with a Google project management certification covering scheduling, risk management and stakeholder coordination. Across the UAE and the UK, I have learned to stay calm under pressure, resolve issues in real time and work seamlessly with multicultural teams. My hospitality background shapes a guest-first approach, and I bring a sharp eye for detail and a genuine passion for creating events people remember.

Working on the floor and studying events taught me to ask three questions after every one: who benefited, who carried the work, and what was left behind. From serving 3,000+ guests at the Dubai World Cup to welcoming delegates at conferences in Belfast, I saw how much of an event happens where the audience never looks.

These four observations follow those questions. One asks how events can leave a destination stronger. One looks at the people behind the stage. One considers how technology should serve the live moment, not replace it. One follows where event money actually goes.

They are honest reflections, not formal research, each short enough to read in one sitting. Whether you plan events, work in them, or simply attend them, you may recognise something here. If a title catches your interest, ask for the full piece and I will send it to you personally.

Beyond the Crowd: Sustainability in Event Tourism

Event tourism is usually judged by crowds and ticket sales. This piece looks at what lies beyond them: environmental care, community balance, cultural respect and local economic value. It connects CSR, ESG and B Corp thinking to everyday event practice, and argues that a strong event should still make sense after the crowd has gone.

Behind the Event: Labour, Livelihoods and the Workforce

Every event rests on a workforce that rarely appears in the programme. This piece looks at who works events, how gig-style contracts shape their security, where the money goes, and why collective voice is hard to build. It closes on a simple question: what do the people behind the event carry home?

Events of Tomorrow: Technology, Tourism and the Human Future of Gathering

Artificial intelligence, immersive design and smart systems are reshaping events. This piece argues that technology should support the live moment, not replace it. It considers personalised journeys, trust, sustainability and local communities, and concludes that the human need to gather will outlast any tool.

Event Tourism and Local Economic Impact

Events bring visitors, spending and attention, but benefit is never automatic. This piece explains direct, indirect and induced impact, then weighs jobs and local business opportunity against leakage, infrastructure risk and short-term thinking. It ends with practical recommendations and a view on event tourism in least developed countries.

My MSc in International Event Management gave me the academic grounding behind the work: how events are planned, led and shaped by strategy and people. Every event teaches something, but a few stay with you. These are the ones that shaped how I work: from my first night behind the scenes as an intern at Meydan, to the grand occasions I later helped deliver, and the conferences that built my foundation in the UK.

Sensation was my first large-scale event. It is the world’s leading electronic music dance event, which began in Amsterdam and was hosted at Meydan, drawing music lovers from around the world. It was about far more than the music: it was about creating a night people would remember for life.

As an intern, I looked after the green room and was the point of contact for artists, their managers and other stakeholders. It was the first time I saw how a seamless night depends on calm, clear communication behind the scenes.

The Dubai World Cup is my primary event. One of the richest horse racing events in the world, it is the grand finale of the Dubai World Cup Carnival at Meydan Racecourse, a prestigious occasion that welcomes guests from across the globe.

I delivered front-line service for VIPs, stakeholders and international delegates, handled allergen and dietary requests, and resolved issues in real time. I also coordinated guest flow and seating across a high-volume event.

This is where my hospitality career took its first steps. A one-time sporting spectacle at the Meydan Grandstand, it saw well-known drivers drift through a vast, demanding course in front of an enormous crowd. It was pure adrenaline.

I supported guest service as part of the hospitality team, working at the pace of a live sporting event beside experienced colleagues. It taught me early how energy and precision go together on event day.

These two events gave me my core exposure to event delivery in the UK. The CABS Annual Conference was a multi-day academic conference in Belfast, and the Gastronomy Summit took place at Ulster University, both bringing together speakers, academics and industry guests.

I welcomed, registered and checked in delegates and speakers, guided guests between sessions, and prepared rooms and materials. I resolved issues in real time to keep each programme to time.

Beyond these, I have been part of many other events, including the Telecom Review Summit, BAO Festival, Endurance Gala Dinner, Skechers Exhibition, Arab Fashion Week, Insomnia Dubai and product launches. Each one added something new, and together they taught me that a great event is built on preparation, teamwork and calm in the moment. I carry that into every event I work on, and I look forward to the next one.
